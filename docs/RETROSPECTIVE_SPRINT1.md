## Sprint 1
### What Went Well
#### Commitment to Delivery: Despite initial delays, the team showed great resilience and increased the development pace significantly to ensure the product increment was ready.

#### Core Functionalities: We managed to move critical features like Job Discovery and Status Controllers into the final stages.

##### Tooling: The GitHub Kanban board was essential to visualize which tasks were lagging behind.

### What Could Be Improved
#### Time Management: We struggled with poor time management at the beginning of the sprint, which led to an uneven distribution of work.

#### Workload Spiking: Because of the slow start, we had to "rush" the development (accelerate the pace) in the final days to meet the deadline, which is not sustainable for long-term quality.

#### Initial Estimation: We underestimated the time required for setup and initial architecture, causing a bottleneck in the middle of the sprint.

### Strategy and Verifiable Action Points
To ensure a more balanced workflow and avoid last-minute rushes in Sprint 2, the team will implement the following strategies:

#### Early Start Policy & Milestone Tracking
The development of all "Must-have" PBIs must start within the first 48 hours of the sprint cycle. We will verify this by auditing the commit history timestamps during the first week of Sprint 2 to ensure work is distributed evenly.

#### Mid-Sprint Internal Review
We will conduct a formal internal progress sync halfway through the sprint to assess our velocity. The verifiable output will be a brief "Status Update" note registered in GitHub Discussions, documenting if the current pace is sufficient to meet the deadline.

#### Granular PBI Breakdown
To prevent large tasks from stalling, any User Story exceeding 5 story points must be broken down into smaller sub-tasks that can be completed in 1–3 days. Success will be verified by ensuring no single task in the "In Progress" column remains stagnant for more than 72 hours.
